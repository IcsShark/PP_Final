# E2: thread scaling and phase-cost ideals

Run the shared serial queue on the cluster with `module load pp`, then
`nohup python3 scripts/exp/e1_driver.py >> results/e1_delta_sweep/driver.log 2>&1 &`.
It delegates to `e2_driver.py`, holds the original E1 lock, uses `run -c 12`, and
never submits while the account has an active job. Do not start a second driver.
`results/e1_delta_sweep/PAUSE_AFTER_JOB` requests a safe exit between jobs.
For a legacy driver without that hook, pause **only the login parent** with
SIGSTOP, let its current compute job finish, then use
`python3 scripts/exp/e2_transition.py OLD_PID CURRENT_JOB_ID`. This refuses to
replace it unless the scheduler is empty and the completed job validates.

Each graph becomes E2-eligible only after its entire E1 manifest completes and
CSV hashes/verification pass. Eligible E2 jobs take priority over remaining E1.
Per-graph plans in `results/e2_scaling/<graph>.json` are immutable and resumable.
The globally fastest E1 OMP timing point chooses one variant/filter. Holding that
pair fixed, independently find the best t6/t12 Δ, include each anchor and its
immediate lower/upper **power-of-two sweep** values, and deduplicate. An exact
non-sweep anchor retains its exact value and uses its bracketing sweep points.
At sweep boundaries only available neighbours are included. Timing selection is
the mean of four source-wise medians, not stats-build time.

Workers reuse E1's four fixed source IDs, use the default schedule, and verify
every invocation. Timing uses three repetitions; stats uses one. Blocks contain
at most two Δ values, all four sources, one thread count and one build, with the
same 1190-second outer cap as E1. Failures stop and flag the queue; oversized
blocks require explicit splitting rather than silently dropping measurements.
Phase logs are reduced in the compute worker and gzipped under
`~/devin/results_raw/e2/`. Only small per-run `.phases.json` reductions are synced
and committed. No login-node phase-log scan is needed.

Synchronize E2 plans, CSVs, `.done`, `.phases.json`, and META; keep complete E1
results locally for baseline/selection validation. Then run:

```sh
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 scripts/exp/e2_test.py
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 -m unittest discover -s analysis
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 analysis/e2_analysis.py
```

The analysis reuses the globally best E1 delta-seq and normal-build Dijkstra;
neither is rerun. Times are mean source-medians. `scaling_table.{csv,md}` selects
the fastest candidate Δ at each thread count. `all_runs.csv` preserves every
Δ/thread point. Efficiency is reported against seq and, separately, OMP t1 at
the same Δ. The all-graph Q2 figure places speedup, efficiency, phases and average
source-normalized `edges_scanned/phases` together. Q3 figures hold Δ fixed and
compare measured OMP-t1 speedup with both phase-log models, avoiding a sequential
baseline mismatch.

For each measured stats run, retain all six costs
`T_k = sum(max(ceil(edges/k), max_vertex_edges))` and the costs without the maximum.
Zero-edge phases contribute zero but remain in phase counts. Each reported ideal
is the arithmetic mean of four source-specific `T_1/T_k` ratios, evaluated on that
thread count's own phase decomposition. Parallel execution can change that
decomposition; the ideal is not an absolute wall-time prediction. E0 observed a
guest topology of **12 cores × 1 thread**; do not presume six physical cores or
attribute a measured gap to memory latency alone (synchronization also contributes).

Outputs: `results/e2_scaling/{all_runs.csv,scaling_table.csv,scaling_table.md,ANALYSIS_META.json}`;
`results/plots/e2_q2_scaling.{pdf,png}` and `e2_<graph>_q3_ideal.{pdf,png}`.
The optional perf experiment is not part of the mandatory queue.
`python3 scripts/exp/e2_status.py` prints a lightweight progress/ETA snapshot;
its E1 extrapolation excludes future E2 and scheduler/NFS overhead.
